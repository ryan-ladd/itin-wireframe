Itin Brand Guidelines

Itin combines sleek design, a smart travel-planning philosophy, and intelligent features into a fun, collaborative space to plan any kind of trip. We want to make travel planning more fun, personalized, and efficient. Itin makes the planning process as beautiful as the trip itself.

Brand pillars

Anyway

Itin puts design first in the planning process. Customize the vibe of your trip.

Anyone

Itin is the super-powered shared planning doc. Collaborate with your travel crew.

Anywhere

Itin is designed for the on-the-go traveler. Add great finds wherever you are.

Logo

The Itin logo is inspired by a continuous route with start, middle, and end points and a detour along the way.

Clear space

Keep a margin around the logo equal to the height of the "t" on every side. Nothing else (type, edges, photos) should enter it.

Minimum size

To keep the Itin logo crisp and legible, never display it smaller than:

Digital: 48 px wide
Print: 0.75 in (20 mm) wide

Below these sizes, the line weight and the three dots begin to blur. When space is tighter, use the app icon instead of shrinking the logo.

Please don't

Stretch or squash it
Rotate it
Use off-brand colors
Place it on busy or low-contrast backgrounds

Imagery

Travel photography is the hero. It should feel like it was taken by a friend who has a good eye.

Photography guidelines

Subject

Real places, food and everyday moments you would stumble on while traveling: street life, landmarks, wildlife and quiet corners. People are candid and usually small in the frame, never posed or looking at the camera.

Light

Natural and moody. Golden hour, dusk, blue hour and neon at night. Let shadows go deep and let highlights glow.

Color

Rich and true to the place. One bold color often leads the frame, like a red lighthouse, a neon sign or a pink building. Edit lightly and keep skin and skies natural.

Framing

Vertical or horizontal, whichever suits the scene. Fill the frame, layer foreground and background for depth, and look for strong lines like arches, rooftops and rows of windows.

Details

Get close to textures and small things: a plate of food, a market stall, tiled walls, rows of windows. Close-ups sit comfortably next to wide scenes and help tell the story of a place.

Avoid

Posed group shots, flat midday light, heavy filters or HDR, cluttered scenes with no clear subject, and anything with logos or watermarks.

Voice & tone

Warm, clear and a little adventurous.
